Part of my consulting practice is helping companies take the intellectual property scattered across their expertise and organize it into something commercializable — easy to understand, easy to consume, easy to sell and say yes to, and easy to scale. At the time, I was trying to advance my own business beyond "website developer" or "campaign planner" into that same territory. So I did what I ask my clients to do: I sat down and worked the problem, end to end.

The result — [red-ideation-session](https://github.com/redrhino-online/red-ideation-session) — is a repository holding a long working session between me and an AI system: prompting, pushing back, renaming, restructuring, until a proprietary model crystallized. That model later became known as the **RED Method**, the framework at the center of my consulting practice ever since. Following my own medicine, so to speak.

## What the session worked out

The session began with the observation that my workshop's content fell into three domains — understanding the customer, the funnel, and traffic — and iterated relentlessly: renaming phases so the acronym became **R.E.D.**, then sharpening what each letter covered:

- **R — Reveal Your Offer.** Identifying the perfect client, clarifying the offer's *currency* (the actual value clients walk away holding, not the features), crafting unique marketing messages, and organizing a coaching curriculum a stranger could follow.
- **E — Engage and Convert.** Developing an effective lead magnet, building a sales funnel with landing and thank-you pages, and teaching a prescriptive sales process — persuasive and pressure-free.
- **D — Develop Your Audience.** A content strategy for social media and email, retargeting and remarketing to make the most of every visitor, and paid advertising that amplifies a proven funnel instead of hoping to rescue an unproven one.

From there the session expanded each stage into transformations (*before* → *after*), defined the four client archetypes the method serves (from the resistant traditionalist to the enlightened master), consolidated ten modules in sequence, specified the done-for-you deliverables — a visual model of the client's process, a custom lead magnet, a training video script, an enrollment blueprint, a 27-email nurture sequence — and closed with the ten KPIs I'd use to prove the engagement worked before and after.

## Rebuilding the artifact

The raw session was valuable but unreadable — 1,620 lines of prompt/response back-and-forth. As an artifact it was a transcript; as a *presentation* it was unusable. So I rebuilt the repository: the original transcript is archived intact under `sources/`, the README now explains the project, and the centerpiece is a **[Reveal.js presentation](https://redrhino-online.github.io/red-ideation-session/)** that turns the session into what it actually was — a consultation.

The deck's conceit: the consultant and the client are both on stage, working the method in dialogue. The client arrives with "marketing feels like guesswork"; the consultant diagnoses three problems wearing a trench coat; each act plays out as conversation, closing with the transformation it produces. **Both roles are played by the same practitioner** — because that's what the session was: me asking the questions my clients would ask, and answering with the framework I'd later sell. Following my own medicine, in both voices at once.

## What it taught me

Two lessons, one methodological and one about AI. Methodological: a proprietary framework isn't written — it's *iterated* into existence. The RED acronym didn't arrive in the first draft; it emerged after the phases were renamed once, then sharpened again, each pass forcing the ideas to earn their place. That iteration is the actual product of an ideation session, and it's why the session is worth preserving.

About AI: the back-and-forth in that repository is a real record of how the method took shape — the AI generating, me steering, both of us boring in on the structure. The value wasn't in any single response; it was in the *sequence* of pushes. Which is also why the presentation works as a dialogue: the method was literally developed as a dialogue, and replaying it that way is more honest than pretending it arrived on a whiteboard, fully formed.
